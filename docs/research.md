# Research: best-in-class sites for kids' theater and class registration

September 2026. Compiled for the Little Characters redesign.

**Method.** Web search only. The build environment blocked direct page fetches, so no site was opened or screenshotted. Every URL below turned up in a search result in September 2026. Features, policies and platforms come from those results. Look-and-feel notes (fonts, colour, imagery) come from background knowledge; check them on the live sites before quoting them.

## Patterns for the marketing site

1. **Organize by age first, with named journeys.** (SCT, School of Rock, Second City) → *Built:* age picker in the hero, age filter in the finder, and a four-step pathway on the home page.
2. **"Find a class" in the header on every page, plus a quick age filter on the home page.** → *Built.*
3. **Deciding details on every card:** age, days and times, place, price, spots left, and whether it ends in a show. (Magnet, Brooklyn Children's Theatre, Dad's Garage) → *Built.*
4. **Show the class ladder.** (Second City, School of Rock) → *Built:* the "Where does my kid start?" risers.
5. **Lead with what parents care about**, and publish helpful articles for search. (Second City) → *Partly built:* "What they'll practice" on each class; articles are future work.
6. **Make the performance a promise.** → *Built:* "★ Ends in a show" on cards; a "The finale" fact on each program.
7. **Local landing pages** for Athens and Winterville. (Alliance) → *Future:* for SEO.
8. **Put trust signals up front:** ratios, caps, teacher bios, inclusion, the founder's story. → *Built.*
9. **Show affordability before checkout:** scholarships, discounts, payment plans. → *Built:* on every program page and the Give page.
10. **Seasonal sale moments and email capture.** (Alliance, Dad's Garage) → *Built:* "Be first in line for summer camp" signup and "Notify me" on upcoming sessions.
11. **Vivid program names** with the age attached. (Filament, Treehouse)
12. **One parent handbook / FAQ** instead of policies scattered through checkout. → *Built:* `/faq`.

## Patterns for enrollment and the portal (Phase 2)

1. Once a child's birthday is saved, the finder shows what fits them ("fits Maya (6)").
2. Browse without an account; identify by email at checkout with a magic link or code; no passwords; stay signed in for a long time; match by email so no one gets a duplicate account.
3. Fill in the household once (guardians, kids, allergies, pickup people, photo consent, saved card) and confirm it once a year.
4. Several children and several sessions in one cart, with sibling and multi-class discounts shown as they apply.
5. Flexible payment: full, deposit or installments; Apple Pay and Google Pay; scholarships applied automatically.
6. Smart waitlist: the button changes in place, the family sees their position, and an open spot is held for 24–48 hours with one tap to claim it.
7. Early access for returning families and donors, set on the account rather than handed out as a code.
8. A confirmation that does the work: add-to-calendar links, what to bring, directions, reminders, and showcase details.
9. Self-service changes within the rules (transfers, cancellations, credits).
10. The family dashboard as the portal's home page: upcoming sessions for every child, the balance in plain words, forms still to complete, and photos and notes from class.

## Things to design out (what Studio Director-style flows get wrong)

- A jump from the website to a different domain with a different look and its own account.
- A login wall before you can see schedules and prices.
- Passwords for families who log in twice a year.
- Duplicate accounts; one child per order; registration only during office hours.
- Unclear money: fees that appear only at the end, and balances nobody can read.
- One task spread across too many screens.
- A hidden, manual waitlist.
- Schedules published only as PDFs.
- Mixing up age and grade: say "age on Sept 1".
- Cutting the emotional features (photos, notes, showcase moments) to make booking faster.
